Time, kept in moving colour.

Two big hands over a living background, and nothing else on the screen.

Version

How it moves

  • The liquid churns hardest around the hands, most of all at their tips.
  • In the last ten seconds of every minute it builds, peaks as the minute turns, then settles.
  • Midnight is different. For ten minutes before, the swirl spreads across the whole screen. At 12:00 it cuts to black, and the clock slowly finds its way back over the next minute.
  • Dawn in Paris is different again, wherever you are. The liquid builds three times, each one cut short, then the screen burns white as the sun comes up, gives way to a long wash of orange morning light, and the clock slowly finds its way back over the next minute.
  • Touch the centre of the clock to open this screen. Nothing else is a button.

Keeping it on display

iPhone: in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, and open the clock from its icon so it runs full screen. Turn the phone sideways on a stand.

Android: in Chrome, open the menu and choose Add to Home screen or Install app.

After your first touch the clock asks the screen to stay on. If your phone still dims, set Auto-Lock (iPhone) or Screen timeout (Android) to a longer time while the clock is on display.
